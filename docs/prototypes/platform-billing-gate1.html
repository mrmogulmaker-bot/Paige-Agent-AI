Two billing domains, kept apart

Money that comes into the platform on the left. Money a workspace collects from its own customers on the right. No table, screen, or Stripe object crosses the line.

Platform Billing

Solo workspace → PAIGE · Settings → Billing · operator configuration
  • Base Solo subscription
  • Included platform capabilities and allowances
  • Verified usage-based charges — only if later approved
  • Phone number / telephony platform charges — only if later approved
  • Paid Marketplace add-ons
  • Platform invoices, payment methods, subscription status, account credits, entitlement
Belongs to the workspace, never to a staff member's personal account. Tables (LAYER 1): platform_subscription_plans · platform_subscriptions · platform_invoices · platform_usage_events · marketplace_installs. Stripe: PAIGE's account.

Client Billing

Solo's customer → Solo business · Sales and future client-finance workflows
  • Invoices, quotes, payments
  • Client balances
  • Related Sales workflows
  • May use the Offer Catalog as its commercial source
Not this workstream. Not modelled, migrated, or implied inside Settings → Billing. Tables (LAYER 2): tenant_products · tenant_prices · tenant_orders · tenant_service_subscriptions. Money leg: the tenant's own processor; PAIGE is never merchant of record for a tenant→client transaction.

Known mocks and unverified behaviour
  • Plan, status, price, renewal: today's real read returns plan/status/price/renewal for a solo workspace, and trialing is a real status; the $74.50 paid beta plan, the 30-day beta trial offer, and promotional grants do not exist as backend contracts — all three are prototype/design truth until Foundation B builds the entitlement model.
  • Portal entry: the shipped function keys the Stripe customer on the person's email, not the workspace (packet finding A1). The "Mapped" state shows the intended behaviour after Billing Foundation; "Unavailable" is what must show until then.
  • Usage: the only measured meter is AI usage in tokens. Allowance, thresholds, warnings, and "used up" behaviour are proposals pending D5–D8. No estimate is ever shown as usage.
  • Add-ons: every item is a fixture. No catalogue read, no checkout, no webhook. Outcomes are chosen by the reviewer with the mock buttons.
  • Role refusal and account switch: Owner-only is the ruled MVP authority (R2); Admin and Member both see the refusal. The switch is a simulated workspace change.
  • Motion: light fades on state change only; the Reduced control removes them. No haptics are claimed — this is a browser simulation.
